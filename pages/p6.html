Practical 6: Histogram Matching & Specification

Reference: Gonzalez & Woods, Ch 3

Objective

Understand and implement histogram matching (specification) — a generalisation of histogram equalization that transforms a source image so its histogram approximates that of a given target image.

Theory: While histogram equalization maps to a uniform distribution, histogram matching maps to an arbitrary target distribution. The key insight is CDF inversion: if we know the source CDF and the target CDF, we can find the intensity mapping by inverting the target CDF at the source CDF values.
  • Source CDF: \( \text{CDF}_s(r) = \sum_{j=0}^{r} p_s(j) \)
  • Target CDF: \( \text{CDF}_t(z) = \sum_{j=0}^{z} p_t(j) \)
  • Matching: For each \( r \), find \( z \) such that \( \text{CDF}_t(z) \approx \text{CDF}_s(r) \)
$$z = \text{CDF}_t^{-1}\!\bigl(\text{CDF}_s(r)\bigr)$$

Analysis Questions

  1. How does histogram matching differ from histogram equalization in terms of the target distribution? When would you prefer one over the other?
  2. Why does the CDF inversion approach work for histogram matching? What mathematical property of CDFs makes this possible?
  3. When matching to multiple targets, which target produces results most similar to histogram equalization, and why?
  4. In the transfer function analysis, what does the deviation between the target CDF and matched CDF tell you about the quality of the matching?
