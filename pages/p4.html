Theory

The power law (gamma) transformation is a fundamental intensity mapping defined as:

$$s = c \cdot r^\gamma$$

where r is the input intensity (normalized to [0, 1]), s is the output intensity, c is a constant, and γ (gamma) controls the shape of the curve:

  • γ < 1 — Expands dark intensities, compresses bright ones (brightening effect)
  • γ > 1 — Compresses dark intensities, expands bright ones (darkening effect)
  • γ = 1 — Identity transformation (no change when c = 1)

The log transformation \(s = c \cdot \log(1 + r)\) compresses dynamic range and is useful for displaying Fourier spectra and enhancing dark regions.

Analysis Questions

  1. What gamma value produces the best enhancement for a dark, underexposed image? Why does a gamma less than 1 brighten dark regions more than bright regions?
  2. Compare the log transform with gamma = 0.4 correction. In what situations would you prefer one over the other?
  3. CRT monitors have an inherent gamma of approximately 2.2. How does this relate to gamma correction applied to images before display? What would an image look like on a CRT without correction?
  4. Why does the power law transformation with γ > 1 improve contrast in bright, washed-out images?
