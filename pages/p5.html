Practical 5: Histogram Equalization

Open the Python notebook version

Objective

Understand and implement histogram equalization — a technique that enhances contrast by redistributing pixel intensities to approximate a uniform distribution.

Theory: Histogram equalization uses the cumulative distribution function (CDF) as a transfer function. For an image with L intensity levels (0 to L−1) and n total pixels:
  • PDF: \( p(r_k) = \frac{n_k}{n} \)
  • CDF: \( T(r_k) = \sum_{j=0}^{k} p(r_j) \)
  • Output: \( s_k = (L - 1) \cdot T(r_k) \)
$$s_k = (L - 1) \cdot \sum_{j=0}^{k} \frac{n_j}{n}$$

Analysis Questions

  1. Does histogram equalization always produce a perfectly uniform histogram? Why or why not?
  2. Why is the CDF used as the transfer function for histogram equalization? What property of the CDF makes it suitable?
  3. Does histogram equalization always improve image quality? Can you think of cases where it might degrade the result?
  4. Why must the transfer function \( T(r) \) be monotonically non-decreasing? What would happen if it were not?
