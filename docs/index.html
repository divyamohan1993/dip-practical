Practical 2: Impact of Sampling Rate on Spatial Resolution

Objective

To evaluate how reducing the sampling rate (N) affects spatial resolution and introduces visible artifacts.

Theory: Spatial resolution is the smallest discernible detail in an image. Halving the number of samples halves the resolution, causing aliasing and loss of fine detail. This is governed by the Nyquist sampling theorem.

  2. Which loses detail first: textured areas or smooth gradients?
  3. If physical size stays constant while pixels halve, how does DPI change?

Practical 4: Gamma Correction & Power Law Transformations

Objective

To implement power law (gamma) transformations for contrast enhancement.

Theory: The power law transformation \(s = c \cdot r^\gamma\) maps input intensities to output intensities. With \(\gamma < 1\), dark regions are enhanced (brightened). With \(\gamma > 1\), bright regions are compressed (darkened). Setting \(\gamma = 1, c = 1\) gives the identity transform.
